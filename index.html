DocBot: Offline Local RAG — Adaptive Context Budgeting & Project Sentinel

Offline PDF chatbot that runs on an 8GB laptop and is the base for two research papers.

−30% latency, 5.9% hallucination rate

  • Python
  • Ollama
  • pgvector
  • TinyBERT
Details

Technologies: Python, LangChain, Ollama (Qwen3-1.7B), PostgreSQL, pgvector, nomic-embed-text, BM25, TinyBERT, Gradio, Docker

Built a fully offline, privacy-preserving PDF chatbot using Retrieval-Augmented Generation, then used it as the base system for two published research projects (first author on both), each targeting a different weakness of small local RAG pipelines. Everything runs on modest hardware (11th-gen Intel i5, 8GB RAM, CPU only):

  • Local RAG Pipeline: Qwen3-1.7B served through Ollama, with nomic-embed-text embeddings and PostgreSQL + pgvector storage, so no data leaves the machine
  • Ingestion & Storage: PDFs chunked at 512 tokens with 64-token overlap, stored in cleanable, session-based indices
  • Real-Time Streaming & Logging: Token-level response streaming via threaded callbacks, with per-query logs (retrieval depth, tokens, latency) exportable to CSV
Research 1 — Adaptive Context Budgeting (ACB), solo research:
  • Problem: A fixed top-k wastes tokens and latency on easy queries. ACB picks the retrieval depth per query instead.
  • Method: Retrieval stops early based on the similarity gap between consecutive chunks and the entropy of the similarity distribution, and is adjusted by query length.
  • Evaluation: Compared with a fixed top-5 baseline on 3 academic PDFs and 30 questions, scored by 3 independent human judges.
  • Result: Latency cut by ~30% (29.5s to 20.6s) and prompt size by ~38% (733 to 454 tokens), with human-judged F1 of 0.748 against 0.811 for the fixed baseline.
Research 2 — Project Sentinel, team research (3 authors):
  • Problem: Small models hallucinate when retrieval returns plausible but irrelevant “distractor” chunks.
  • Semantic Gatekeeper: A fine-tuned TinyBERT cross-encoder scores each (query, chunk) pair for factual utility and discards chunks below the threshold.
  • Early Exit: If no retrieved chunk passes the threshold, the system politely refuses instead of letting the generator answer from unsupported context.
  • Hybrid Retrieval: BM25 keyword search and pgvector dense search run in parallel and are fused with Reciprocal Rank Fusion.
  • Open Artifacts: Built and released a labeled dataset derived from HotpotQA (69k training and 7k validation pairs), plus the fine-tuned model, on Hugging Face.
  • Result: 5.92% hallucination rate at 5.3ms CPU inference latency (ROC-AUC 0.906), at the cost of lower fact recall (60% at the strict threshold).

EGMR: Entropy-Guided Matryoshka Retrieval — Master's Thesis (defended)

Thesis research: routing each biomedical question to a light or a deep retrieval index.

Better retrieval at 30% of the deep-search cost

  • Python
  • FAISS
  • Qdrant
  • RAGAS
Details

Technologies: Python, Ollama (Qwen3-1.7B), nomic-embed-text, FAISS, Qdrant, BM25, RAGAS, MLflow, Podman

Master's thesis research on per-question adaptive retrieval for resource-constrained biomedical RAG. EGMR routes each question between a compact (64-dim) and a full-resolution (768-dim) dense index using a training-free uncertainty signal from a small local model, then stress-tests whether that routing really tracks question difficulty:

  • Dual-Resolution Index: Built from a single Matryoshka embedding pass over 75,919 deduplicated PubMed snippets, served through both FAISS and Qdrant with an in-RAM / on-disk split matching clinic-scale hardware.
  • Margin-Based Routing Gate: A logit-margin uncertainty signal from a no-context draft answer decides, per question, which index to search; calibrated against a declared 30% deep-search compute budget.
  • Full Evaluation: Four generation tiers (zero-shot, oracle, static, routed) over the complete BioASQ Task 14b training set (5,729 questions, 22,916 generation calls), scored with BERTScore and RAGAS using a separate judge model.
  • Diagnostic Audit: Paired Wilcoxon tests, Monte Carlo random-routing controls, bootstrapped AUC and Bonferroni-corrected correlation tests, plus root-cause analysis of five evaluation anomalies.
Key Findings:
  • Significantly better retrieval than an always-shallow baseline (Wilcoxon p<0.0001) at 30% of the deep-search cost.
  • Reported a negative result with equal prominence: the routing signal allocates budget effectively but does not identify which individual questions are hard (AUC ≈ 0.49 against a 0.5 chance baseline).

Legal RAG Assistant: Document Ingestion Pipeline

Turns scanned and bilingual regulatory PDFs into precisely cited, article-level chunks for a legal RAG assistant.

Zero article-numbering collisions on the test corpus

  • Python
  • FastAPI
  • DocTR
  • Podman
Details

Technologies: Python, FastAPI, PyMuPDF, pdfplumber, DocTR (PyTorch), OpenCV, Streamlit, Podman

Internship project at a confidential enterprise client (financial services sector). I designed and built the full ingestion module of an internal legal RAG assistant that answers regulatory questions with citations to the exact article and page, on fully local infrastructure. The retrieval and generation chain was built by the rest of the team. My scope was everything that turns a raw PDF into clean, citable text:

  • Page Classification: Detects digital, scanned, or mixed pages from character-density heuristics and forces OCR when corrupted glyphs are found, so OCR only runs where it is needed.
  • Bicolumn Extraction: Detects French/Arabic two-column layouts by analyzing horizontal word gaps and keeps the right column, with a fallback extractor when the primary output looks corrupted.
  • OCR: DocTR (DBNet + CRNN) chosen over Tesseract for native multi-column handling and better-calibrated confidence scores, with SHA-256-keyed disk caching and per-page confidence carried through to each chunk.
  • Legal-Aware Chunking: Article-level chunks with paragraph and sentence fallbacks, hierarchy tracking (book, title, chapter, section), deduplication, and decree-boundary detection that stops article numbers of an annexed decree from colliding with its parent law.
  • Validation & Human Review: Schema checks on every chunk, plus a two-step admin interface where analysts correct OCR or article-number errors and chunk IDs and citations are recomputed before indexing.
Results:
  • Zero article-numbering collisions across a four-document corpus that includes a fully scanned, degraded document.
  • Full OCR chain validated end to end on the scanned document, including the bicolumn layout.

Describe an emergency in Darija and a drone fleet organizes the medical delivery.

Team project, 4 students

  • Whisper
  • Mesa
  • OpenStreetMap
  • Local LLM
Details

Technologies: Python, Mesa, Solara, Whisper (fine-tuned), local LLM, OpenStreetMap, OpenTopography, Streamlit, Gradio, Pygame

Team project (4 students): a multi-agent drone logistics simulation for emergency medical delivery in rural Morocco, where poor road access can turn a medical emergency fatal. A request spoken in Darija triggers a drone fleet that coordinates delivery on its own:

  • Darija Voice Activation: A fine-tuned Whisper ASR model combined with a local LLM extracts medical intent and destination from natural spoken Darija.
  • Real-World Map Generation: Pick a bounding box anywhere in Morocco and the system maps pharmacies, telecom towers and impassable high-altitude terrain from OpenStreetMap and OpenTopography into the simulation grid.
  • Auction-Based Dispatch: Drones bid on missions using a cost function that balances battery, flight distance and payload urgency.

  • Infrastructure Reuse: Existing rural telecom towers act as mid-flight charging points for the fleet.
